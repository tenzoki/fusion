# Upgrading to v10.20 (from v10.14)

One note for 10.20.0, the citation-form release. No `upgrading-to` note exists for 10.15 through 10.19; `git log v10.14.0..v10.19.2` is the record for those. Nothing here rewrites a file in your project, and no migration step is required. What changes is how a record is cited, and one line in the cleanup report that tells you whether your own corpus follows the form.

## What you will notice

- **A record is cited by its storeless basename**, `YYMMDD-HHMM_*_<topic>.md`; a Circle by its bare directory name; a markerless artifact (history, review, analysis) as `YYMMDD-HHMM-<topic>.md`. A citation that still carries a store segment (`shared/issues/...`, `circles/<dir>/decisions/...`, `circles/` in front of a Circle) is a violation, because the segment is what an archive sweep moves. The rule is `rules/fusion-workbench-conventions.md` `## Filename Patterns`; the reader resolves a citation with one workbench-wide `find`. Decisions `260828-0904_*_does-the-mandated-citation-form-include-the-store-segment.md` and `260828-0904_*_is-an-archived-record-a-citation-target.md`.
- **`bin/fusion-citation-check` is new.** It runs the same grammar fusion's own gates run over your live workbench plus `CLAUDE.md`, `rules/*.md`, `.claude/rules/*.md` and `docs/**/*.md`, and prints `KEY=value` counts ending in `verdict=clean|violations`, then one row per unresolved or store-prefixed citation. The verdict never reaches the exit code; it reports and gates nothing. `--undecidable` adds the bare stamps, which name a minute and not a file. Decision `260828-0904_*_does-fusion-ship-a-citation-checker-to-consuming-projects.md`.
- **`/fusion:cleanup` Step 8 prints that verdict** as a `Citations:` bullet (the `verdict=`, `store-prefixed=` and `dangling=` lines). Until you run `fusion --update`, the installed copy has no such helper and the bullet reads `citations: helper-missing`; that is the one-release-behind cost every new `bin/` helper pays, not a fault in your project.
- **Two tools, and only one of them writes.** `bin/fusion-citation-check` is the one for a consumer's everyday use: it reports, `/fusion:cleanup` repeats its verdict, and it never touches a file. `bin/fusion-citation-sweep` is the rewriter, and no fusion pipeline or skill ever runs it; you run it by hand, `--dry-run` first (the default: the census per file, then the bare stamps it will not judge, since a stamp names a minute and not a file, and no option makes it guess). `--write` refuses unless your workbench is tracked in a clean git work tree, so the sweep is its own commit and the way back is one revert; it prints the census and stops unless you also pass `--yes`. It rewrites only the token span, never a fenced or block-quoted token, and never a file outside the workbench unless you name one. `--repair` undoes what the 10.20.0 script's bare-stamp rule did to a workbench it touched, behind the same guards. Decision `260829-1623_*_does-fusion-ship-the-citation-sweep-or-only-the-checker-and-under-which-guards.md`.
- **The archive safety filter matches the wildcard form.** A record cited as `<stamp>_*_<slug>.md` in your shipped text is now kept out of a sweep, where before only the literal marker spelling was seen (issue `260828-0901_*_the-archive-safety-filter-greps-the-literal-basename-and-cannot-match-the-wildcard-citation-form-the-rule-mandates.md`).
- **Shipped prompts no longer say a fusion record sits in your resolver's decision or issue store.** Those store keys resolve to your workbench, where fusion's own records never were; the lines now say the record is fusion's own, and a lint keeps a record stamp and a store key off one line (decision `260828-0904_*_are-shipped-record-citations-provenance-or-pointers-for-a-consuming-agent.md`).

## Verify after updating

`fusion --update`, restart, then `"$FUSION_PLUGIN_ROOT/bin/fusion-citation-check"` at your project root: it prints `anchor=workbench-root` and a `verdict=` line. `store-prefixed=` is the count the sweep would fix; `dangling=` names citations no record matches, which the sweep cannot fix and which are usually a typo or a record that was deleted rather than archived.
