# Is the em-dash ceiling read per file, or across the always-on corpus as a whole?

## Question

The rule states one em-dash per 1000 words. Read across the six always-on files together that permits
13 marks. Read per file it permits 10, and permits **zero** in `rules/agent-setup.md`, which carries
502 prose words. Both readings satisfy the Circle's Directive; they differ in strictness and in what
they promise about a future short rule file.

## Options

1. **Corpus-level**, with per-file rates reported. The shaper's recommendation. The Directive's own
   wording says "the always-on rule corpus sits at or under the ceiling".
2. **Per file**, with the corpus total reported beside it. The analyst's recommendation, on the ground
   that both authored surfaces state the rule of a single document: `stilwerk/default-voice-en.yaml`
   entry AI02 addresses a writer of one document, and the readability gate in
   `rules/user-facing-output.md` speaks of "your draft".

## Answer: option 2, per file.

Two reasons. The evidence is one-sided: no authored surface states the rule of a collection, and both
that do state it of a document. And per-file is the stricter test, so meeting it also meets the
corpus reading, which means the Circle cannot be found later to have satisfied the weaker reading of
an ambiguous rule.

## What this forecloses

A future always-on rule file shorter than about 1000 words may carry no em-dash at all, because its
allowance rounds to zero. That is the rule as written, and the alternative is a ceiling that depends
on how the corpus happens to be split into files.
