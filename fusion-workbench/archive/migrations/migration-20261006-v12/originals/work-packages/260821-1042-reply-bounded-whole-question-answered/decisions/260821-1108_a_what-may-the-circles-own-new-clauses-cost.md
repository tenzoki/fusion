# What may the Circle's own new clauses cost?

---
**Domain:** code
**Filed by:** orchestrator
**Cross-references:** `260821-1042-reply-bounded-whole-question-answered`

---

## Question

The Circle that preceded this one repaired the register of the always-on corpus and grew it: 1 939
bytes of new style clauses against 470 bytes the repunctuation returned, a ratio of 4.1 to 1, and
2 138 bytes net once a layout row on another subject is counted. Nothing bounded that, because the
Directive named four outcomes and no cost.

This Circle's subject is saying less. A Circle that says "say less" in more words teaches the register
it forbids.

## Options

1. **Net zero or less.** The always-on corpus may not grow; every new clause is paid for by a cut.
2. **A fixed byte budget** the planner holds and states in the plan head.
3. **Report, do not bound.** Each step names its bytes, no ceiling.

## Answer: option 1, net zero or less.

## What this forecloses

A clause may not be longer even where the shorter form is worse. That cost is real and was accepted at
the gate: the alternative is option 3, which is exactly what the preceding Circle did, and it grew by
2 138 bytes while nobody was watching a number.

This also means the Circle carries a cut in its own plan, and the cut has to come from the same corpus
it is repairing. The open decision
`260821-0414_*_does-a-corpus-repair-circle-carry-a-budget-for-what-its-own-new-clauses-may-spend.md`
asks the general form of this question and is answered here only for this Circle.
