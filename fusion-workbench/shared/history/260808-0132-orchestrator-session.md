The user asked whether the remaining defects could be fixed autonomously. The honest answer was no,
and the estimate given before reading was also wrong.

Four defects were named as mechanical from their titles. Reading the bodies cut that to three.
`260807-1942_*_die-domaenenerkennung-entscheidet-vor-der-codezaehlung-und-erreicht-code-nie.md` (the domain-detection branch order) and `260807-1951_*_die-tiefenschranke-der-codezaehlung-sieht-keinen-cargo-workspace.md` (the code-count depth bound) each
close with a section titled "Was zu entscheiden ist, aber nicht hier" — both records refuse to name a
correction and say the form belongs in a decision record. Classifying them from titles was the error;
they are the two records in that set that most explicitly are not fixes.

That reclassification is worth keeping, because those two are the reason a consuming project's domain
detection has been overridden by hand in at least four sessions across five days. A measurement every
operator discards is not a heuristic any more, and no amount of mechanical fixing addresses that.
