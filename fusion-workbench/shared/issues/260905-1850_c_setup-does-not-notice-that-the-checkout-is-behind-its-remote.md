Setup does not notice that the checkout is behind its remote
---
A session started on 4 September in a checkout last pulled on 29 August, 25 commits and three releases behind `origin/main`, and nothing in Setup said so. The session bumped the version to one that already existed and wrote an upgrade note for it. The presence check reported no other checkouts, which was literally true of what this checkout had pulled, and is the blind spot its own description names.
---
**Filed by:** orchestrator, Kai Stalmann <ks@qantr.com>

**What happened, measured.** `git rev-list --left-right --count origin/main...HEAD` read `25 33` after a fetch on 5 September: 25 commits on the remote this checkout had never seen, 33 made here on top of a stale base. The 25 came from checkout `114caf11` between 30 August and 1 September and included the releases v10.21.0, v10.21.1 and v10.22.0. This checkout, `5e8248d7`, prepared v10.21.0 on 5 September not knowing it existed. The merge that followed conflicted on twelve files and put two growth bounds over.

**Why nothing caught it.** `/fusion:setup` Step 0c runs `bin/fusion-events presence`, which reads the event log this checkout holds and says so: `scope=pulled`, "only what this checkout has pulled, so a session started elsewhere since the last fetch is invisible, not absent". That is honest and it is the wrong question for this case. The question that would have caught it is whether the local branch is behind its upstream, and no step asks it.

**Acceptance.** Setup reports, in its Done report, how many commits the current branch is behind its upstream and when the remote was last fetched, or that no upstream is configured, or that the read could not be taken and why. Whether Setup fetches to answer it, or reads the last-fetched state and names its age, is the implementing agent's to decide and to state: a fetch is a network call at Setup, and a stale answer without its age is worse than none. Advisory, never blocking, like every other Setup check.

Resolved: 260905-1858-coder-session.md `## What changed` — `/fusion:setup` gains Step 0k, which reads how far the current branch is behind its upstream and reports it in the Done report with the age of the view it came from. It reads the local remote-tracking ref and does not fetch: a fetch is the only network call in an otherwise wholly local sequence, it writes refs before the user has agreed to anything, and against a credentialed remote it blocks a non-interactive shell at a prompt with no portable timeout. The cost of that choice is stated rather than hidden. `fetched=never`, the shape this record was filed on, renders as a warning whatever the count says, and above 24 hours the age leads and the count follows. `upstream=none`, `upstream=no-work-tree` and `counts=unread` are each their own rendered case, and no branch prints a zero in place of a read it could not take. Advisory throughout; nothing blocks and nothing asks.
