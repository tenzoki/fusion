## 6. What in `## The fourth subject — work-item edges` could not be followed as written

This is the part of the run worth the most. Eight findings, each met while running the pass rather than while
reading it, ordered by what they cost.

### F7 — two entries writing one line: the apply pass invalidates its own approvals

**Severity: highest. It makes a correct survey produce a wrong apply.** Both edge fields are **single lines**
holding a comma-separated list. Every distinct edge into one field is its own ledger entry by the section's own
rule ("each is its own ledger entry"), and every entry is independently approvable by id. But
`### Pass 2 — apply` is written for entries occupying **disjoint regions**: it re-reads the before-text, and a
mismatch is `stale`. Two approved entries into one field are guaranteed to collide — the first one written
falsifies the second one's before-text.

Not hypothetical and not rare: it fired on the very first run, at a yield of two. Any run proposing two or more
edges into one field of one item hits it.

**What the section would need.** Either the entry is redefined as "one field on one item", with every approved
basename folded into a single After at apply time — which breaks per-id approval, since the After can then not
be written at survey time — or `### Pass 2 — apply` gains a merge rule for these two fields specifically: on a
`stale` whose only difference is that the field gained a basename this entry did not name, re-derive the After
by inserting this entry's target into the line as found. The second is smaller and keeps per-id approval, but it
is an exception to "**never re-derive a proposal in this pass**", which is load-bearing. **This needs a ruling,
not a patch**, and it is the one finding that blocks the feature from real use.

### F3 — the target endpoint is reachable only by a container hop the section does not authorise

`### The classification` requires both endpoints to be **work items**. In practice a live item's prose cites
**records** far more often than work-item basenames: in this corpus, 4 work-item basenames against some 30
record citations. Both of this run's relations were read off a citation of a **record**, and the work item was
reached by resolving the citation and reading which container it landed in.

The hop is supportable — the lookup returns a path, and a container is a work item's directory by construction —
and **the section neither authorises it nor forbids it.** A stricter reader executes the same pass and returns a
yield of one instead of two: a real divergence between two correct readings of one prompt. It also decides the
size of the feature. Without the hop the pass sees 4 candidate names; with it, some 30 citations become
candidates, and the plan's `## Risks & Mitigations` row about flooding the gate with citation proposals stops
being mitigated. **This needs a sentence either way**, and it is the finding most likely to change the feature's
character.
