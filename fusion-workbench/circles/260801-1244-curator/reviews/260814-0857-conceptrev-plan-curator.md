## Verdict

The design these three graphs describe is coherent, and the structural measurements are unusually clean for a plan of this size. All three blocks parse and render. No graph contains a cycle, no node exceeds a fan-out of two, no node is orphaned, and every one of the three sits at an edge-to-node ratio of exactly 1.00, which is tree-shaped rather than dense. The step dependency graph agrees with the step list edge for edge, which is the cross-check that most often fails and here does not. The verdict stops one step short of clean because of the first diagram, and for one reason worth naming at the gate: the section titled "How the gate is crossed" draws the three invocation shapes identically, so the single structural fact that distinguishes them, the two-dispatch round trip on two of the three paths, exists only in edge-label text and not in the shape of the graph. The design underneath is sound. The picture under-reports it.

## Findings

**F1 — Diagram 1 draws three invocation shapes as one shape (Medium, substantive on the diagram, not on the design).** `SKILL`, `TOP` and `ORCH` each attach to `pass1` by a single dotted edge, and to nothing else. Read as a graph, the three paths are indistinguishable: same source cardinality, same target, same style. The prose immediately below says they "differ only in who owns `AskUserQuestion`", and step 3 of the plan makes the difference concrete, since `/fusion:curate` dispatches the agent twice, once with `**Mode:** survey` and once with `**Mode:** apply`. That second dispatch is what drives `pass2`, and no edge in the graph carries it. The apply pass is reachable in the picture only through `gates -->|approved entry ids| VER`, an internal flow that implies one continuous run. The `ORCH` edge label, "returns the gate question, re-dispatched with approvals", is the only place the round trip appears, and an edge label is not graph structure.

Two consequences follow, and they differ in weight. The first is presentational: a reader who reads shape before labels concludes the three shapes are the same, which is the opposite of the section's point. The second deserves an explicit statement so the measurement is not over-read later. The graph's acyclicity is genuine, not an artefact of the omission. A caller dispatching, receiving a return, and dispatching again is a call return, not a bidirectional dependency between two components, so nothing here trips `HYG-NO-CYCLES`. The agent depends on no caller; all three callers depend on the agent.

**F2 — `G2`'s reject branch has no edge (Low, substantive).** `G2{"Approve all, by group, by entry, or reject"}` is drawn as a decision node with four named outcomes and zero outgoing edges of its own. The single exit from the gate leaves the `gates` container, labelled "approved entry ids", which covers the three approving outcomes. Rejection is a fourth outcome the graph does not route anywhere. The plan's own text settles the behaviour, since the ledger is written whether or not anything is applied and `pass2` runs over approved entries only, so a rejection ends the run at the ledger. Drawing that terminal edge would cost one line and would close the one branch a reader has to infer.

**F3 — Diagram 1 mixes container-level and node-level edge endpoints (Low, cosmetic).** Two of the three subgraphs are used as edge endpoints, `LED --> gates` and `gates --> VER`, while `pass2` is entered at node level and never referenced as a container. Within the `gates` cluster this also blurs which node the edges actually meet: entry belongs to `G1` and exit belongs to `G2`, and the graph attaches both to the boundary. The rendering is legible either way. The inconsistency is a drawing convention, not a design defect, and it is recorded so a later reader does not mistake the isolated `pass2` container identifier for an orphan.

**F4 — Diagram 2's unlabeled edges are mostly justified, with two exceptions (Low, cosmetic).** Six of the eight edges carry no label, which on a semantic graph would normally be a finding. Here the node labels carry the relation instead, and carry it precisely: `CORE["Universal core = intersection of all emissions"]` and `EXTRA["Role extras = emission minus core"]` state what the two edges out of `EM` mean, so labelling them would duplicate the node text. The two edges out of `BASE` are the exception. Nothing in the graph says that `RULE_BASELINE` supplies the per-file floor to each `growth()` call rather than being a second input of the same kind as the file set, and three words on each edge would fix it. Worth noting on the other side: the escaping of `<` and `>` as `&gt;` inside the threshold labels is correct Mermaid, and it is what let both threshold edges survive rendering.

**F5 — The absence of subgraphs in diagrams 2 and 3 is defensible (no action).** The authoring rule asks for `subgraph` blocks and an explicit direction so layering is visible. Both graphs declare a direction and neither groups. At eight nodes forming four clean left-to-right columns, and six nodes forming a four-level diamond, the direction alone produces the layering, and a grouping box would add a frame without adding a tier. Recorded so the measurement is not read as a rubric miss.

**F6 — Diagram 3 agrees with the step list exactly (no action, and it is the check that usually fails).** The graph's six edges reproduce every declared dependency and add none: step 2 on step 1, steps 3 and 4 on step 2, step 5 on steps 3 and 4. The `V` node is labelled "not an executor step", which matches `## Validation Run (C11)` naming C11 as a user-invoked run after step 5 rather than an assigned step. The critical path the graph shows, five nodes from `S1` to `V`, is the path the plan schedules. On the last three plans this evaluator has measured, a dependency stated in the step list and missing from the DAG was the recurring defect. It is absent here.

**F7 — No structural content in the document lacks a diagram (no action).** Three candidates were considered and each is correctly undrawn. The three evidence tiers and their decidability split are the spec's content, and the plan states in its Directive that it does not restate the spec. The ledger entry and run file in `## Data Structures` are a field list and a containment of depth two, which a table carries better than an `erDiagram`. The five mechanical gates are a lookup, not a graph. The document describes what is worth seeing and does not pad.

## What a clean redraw would require

Not applicable. The verdict is acceptable, not tangled, and none of the findings above calls for a design change. F1 and F2 are completeness gaps in one picture, each closable by adding edges to the existing graph rather than by rethinking anything the graph shows. For F1 the cheapest honest fix is a second dotted edge from `SKILL` and from `ORCH` into `pass2`, which makes the two-dispatch shape visible and leaves `TOP` as the single-edge case it genuinely is. A `sequenceDiagram` covering the three invocation protocols would show the round trip more exactly, at the cost of a fourth diagram in a plan that is not short. The single-edge addition is proportionate; the sequence diagram is optional.
